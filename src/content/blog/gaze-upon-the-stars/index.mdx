## Prelude: What's a "Home Paige"? {#whats-a-home-paige}

import SimpleIconsSteam from "~icons/simple-icons/steam";

<Alert type="note">
	If you don't need to know what Home Paige is, you can go ahead and jump to the [next section
	here](#chapter-1-the-song-itself).
</Alert>

_Home Paige_ is a **platformer** game created in Unreal Engine, by the indie game studio _Minimin_.
The game is set to take place in a 90s operating system. At the time of writing it's **not out**
yet, however Minimin hopes to bring out Chapter 1 somewhere in the **second half of 2026**[^1].

In the meantime, there is a little **tech / movement demo** that you can try out on <Link
	href="https://store.steampowered.com/app/4098960/Home_Paige/" external><SimpleIconsSteam /> Steam</Link>.
It only natively supports **Windows**, but it _should_ also run perfectly fine on **Linux** with the
help of **Proton**. <small>(You never know though, because it's Linux. We've come a long way, but
there still is room for improvement.)</small> The final game will have proper **Mac** and **Linux**
versions[^1].

</small>

Now, what do I like so much about it, as to spend a lot of my time making covers of its soundtrack
and such?

1. It's got a high paced, intricate movement system that is quite fun to play around with, even if
   my skill issue doesn't let me make the best use of it. <i class="text-dim">It took me an
   embarrassingly long amount of time to get that screenshot...</i>

2. The game has a quite playful art style, with graphics that are very much reminiscent of early 3D
   platformers, while also being semi-modern at the same time <small>(which makes sense, considering
   it's an Unreal Engine game)</small>. It's definitely something special.

3. The soundtrack also captures the same playful vibe, making use of instruments ranging from chip
   sounds to synthesizers to straight up MIDI instruments. That last one's quite fitting if you
   consider how prevalent MIDI files were in the time period this game's taking you back to.

   <small>
   	If you want to hear more of this wonderful OST, check out [this handy dandy
   	playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL7NztWyzeIiFOc2GFmxYc8nSaMjvSqQY6) created by
   	Home Paige Discord member Salmo! It includes a bunch of songs that Minimin hasn't released on
   	YouTube yet, along with some covers and fan songs that are inspired by it.
   </small>

4. There are a lot of hidden references, such as the Pipes stage looking like the 3D Pipes
   screensaver from the olden days, and the MIDI sounds I just mentioned. Some even connected parts
   of the OST with old startup sounds and menu music. Heck, it even has a song called Startup.

All of that gives the game its charm, which resonates with me quite a bit, but that's just my
opinion. Some of you may have mixed feelings towards it, and if that's how you feel, so be it. If
not, then perhaps you should check out the demo linked above.

that in a bit.

### The Rhythm {#rhythm}

The cover's base tempo is **92 beats per minute**, 4 less than the original's **96**. However, the
final version was actually slowed down by another 4, resulting in a tempo of **88 bpm**. That's also
why the pitch is lower. I go into a bit more detail about this
[towards the end of the article](#gluing-it-all-together).

The instruments are generally locked to <span class="font-serif text-icon">♪</span> 8^th^ notes,
with an exception being the keys during the intro part. The drums, however, are more interesting;
they contain some triplets, but those triplet notes are slightly early, as can be seen in the
pattern below.

### The Sounds {#sounds}

Now it's time to discuss what makes this cover sound the way it does, by going over each of its
tracks (in no particular order). There's quite a bit to go through, so strap in!

#### Keys

This is likely the very first sound you notice. The tone is produced by **Wavetable**, and is
automated to get brighter as the song progresses, as well as a bit quieter to make room for the
melodies. It's also layered with some microcassette noise from Cymatics, which has some hum to it,
that I tuned to match the notes being played.

To top it off, there are some effects like a **chorus**, **Dynamic Tube** for some subtle
distortion, and **Spectral Blur** for some unique reverb.

	src={import("./assets/video/keys-demo.mp4")}

	height={512}

/>

The notes are not quite the same as the original. For instance, during the intro, this instrument is
supposed to keep playing a <strong
	class="font-serif">I^add9^</strong> <span class="font-serif text-dim"> (E^add9^)</span> chord, but
that clashed a little too much with the <strong class="font-serif">iii</strong> <span
	class="font-serif text-dim">(A♭m)</span> chord in this cover. I therefore adjusted those notes to
fit in better.

And for the rest of the song, I only discovered the notes were deviating from the original late into
making the second version of the cover. Changing them to match just didn't sit right with me, so I
left them. This is my cover, after all. Oh, and of course, the last part before the outro was
changed to accommodate the chord switch-up.

#### Arp

This instrument comes in along with the drums. The original uses a synth here sound that glides a
little between every note. In this cover, though, I very quickly ended up with this plucky sound,
which I then added many unique things to. Just look at how much is going on here:

<VideoPlayer
	src={import("./assets/video/arp-demo.mp4")}
	createdIn="2026"
	width={1680}
	height={512}
	loop
/>

See? That was a lot. If you're not already familiar with Ableton Live or music production, I can
imagine you're having trouble understanding all of that. So, let's break it down step by step.

1. We immediately start off with something special. There's an instance of **Chord**, whose
   **Strum** and **Velocity** parameters are being randomized by **Expression Control** for each
   incoming note.

   You don't need to know what that means exactly, only that it randomly sprinkles in some extra
   notes. The **Chance** also increases over time, for even more... sprinklage! <small>_Is that even
   a word?_</small>

2. The sound is again produced by **Wavetable**. It becomes brighter and longer over the course of
   the song.

3. There's an instance of **EQ Eight** to filter the arp during the outro. But that's not very
   important.

4. Now here's something juicy:
   1. We have a **Grain Delay** modulated by an **LFO** to create a burst of grains every 2 bars,
      again automated to get more intense.
   2. There's also a **Chorus** on the other chain to widen the sound some. The grains aren't
      affected by it, but now that I think about it, maybe they should've been...

5. Finally, there's a homemade tape delay effect, appropriately named _Tape Delay at home_:
   1. The first effect in the chain is **iZotope Vinyl**, which brings a touch of lo-fi to the
      delay. Quite literally, in fact, because its **Lo-fi** option is enabled, adding some sampling
      artefacts.
   2. Those artefacts turned out to be a bit too much, though. Thankfully we've got **TDR Nova**
      <small>(a dynamic EQ plugin)</small> to tone 'em down.
      <small>
      	This actually flew under the radar at first, because the headset I normally use (the HyperX
      	Cloud Alpha's) didn't pick them up very well. I did notice them later though, when listening
      	to the song on my phone through my JBL Live 770NC's. I guess this is what happens when you
      	use headphones that are not designed for music production.
      	{/* Oxfmt insists on making this a block for some reason */}
      	<i class="text-dimmer">
      		And yes, I know I could've just used Redux, which has a dry-wet option, but it's too late
      		now.
      	</i>
      </small>
   3. The actual delay effect is provided by **Echo**, configured to add some additional noise,
      wobble and reverb. Oh, and you bet the delay grows as the song progresses!<br />
      <small>
      	Its delay time is also modulated _very_ slightly, although I'm not really sure why I did
      	that.
      </small>

Alright, now you should hopefully understand how this sound works. Another thing that's different is
the fact that it has a **ping-pong delay**, as opposed to the original's simple mono delay. That was
definitely the right move. I just love that ping-pong effect.

The final thing I did was **resample** the sound on a new track, which was then drowned in
**reverb** and **faded in** right before the actual arp kicks in. I repeated this before the start
of the next section as well.

#### Drum...

I wanted the drums to be a lot more prevalent in this cover. The rather short kick-snare-hat loop
that sits in the background of the original Stargazer wasn't going to cut it for that, so I needed
to add some variety.

For starters, I brought in some additional drum sounds: a secondary **offbeat snare**, an **open
hi-hat**, a **ride cymbal**, and lastly, a **reverse crash**. Then, I produced a few extra **drum
fills** (the original only had 2 of them). And of course there's your fair share of effects, like
**reverb**, **saturation** and some automated **filters**.

The drum sounds are a mixture of free **Cymatics** and stock **Ableton** samples. I don't feel
qualified to make my own yet, nor do I really want to spend money on samples. <i
	class="text-dim">(Because why would I do that when I only make free music?)</i>

The... snare, if you want to call it that, also has a little extra click sound that I added by
filtering out the high frequencies and adding delay to that. It also swaps ears with every hit. This
is _incredibly_ subtle though, so if you can hear it, good job!

##### Drum Reverb

I want to briefly touch on the reverb for a bit. It's powered by **Hybrid Reverb**, which is
probably my favourite reverb effect that's built into Live Suite. You'll definitely be seeing more
of it later.

Hybrid Reverb has a **Vintage** slider, which adds effects like sampling artefacts and bitcrush,
quite appropriate for a lo-fi song. Here that slider is set to the second-highest setting of
**Older**, which makes the bitcrush quite noticeable, especially with the saturation that follows
it. Listen closely, and you'll hear what I mean:

#### ...& Bass

I don't really need to talk too much about the bass sounds here. They both use Wavetable, and sound
quite similar to the original song, probably the most similar of any sound in this cover. And that's
simply because they work well. There's no need to change them too much.

There are just a few things I want to mention. The reese-like bass that plays _almost_ the entire
time has a fair bit of **subtle randomness** going on, especially from the **Shimmer unison
algorithm**. The softer, higher-up fills are also actually a separate sound that uses **chorus**
instead of unison.

And lastly, both sounds make use of a **sub oscillator** for a bit of extra oomph. Not too much,
though, because remember, it is lo-fi. Bass frequencies were rather difficult to produce in the time
periods we try to emulate with lo-fi music.

I usually add **sidechain** to bass sounds to avoid muddying up the mix too much. And while I
technically did do that here, the pump effect you'd associate with sidechain doesn't fit in here
very well, so it never ends up reducing the volume by more than about **2 dB**.

#### Strings

This sound is made up of 2 layers. First is actually some real strings from the stock Ableton
**String Quartet** pack, that are played <small>incredibly softly</small>. They're pretty subtle in
the full mix; the intro is probably the part where you notice them the most. Second is yet another
**Wavetable** synth, with a tone that was very mildly inspired by
[Andrew Huang's HOLY](https://youtube.com/watch?v=8mAviAh3Tzg). <small><small>(big fan by the
way)</small></small>

You may find this hard to believe <small>(or you may not)</small>, but my sound design skills are
not quite up to his level. So instead, we've got some effects to compensate: a **Phaser** and a
**Spectral Resonator**. Together they contribute to this sort of "slushy" sound that this second
version of the cover ended up with.

#### The Final Melody

The song has been building up to this moment. It better sound good.

Well... as it turns out, that wasn't really the case at first. It used to be on the same octave as
the original, and that ended up sounding pretty grating; enough to turn a few people off. And it
used to be even worse too, which made someone in the
[Home Paige Discord server](https://discord.gg/homepaige) complain about it when I shared
[the very first early version of the cover](https://discord.com/channels/1414500915200917597/1458911508464537641/1458911508464537641).

It was clear to me that this sound needed some changes. So in this new version, it has been dropped
down an octave and toned down a bunch, along with a few more tweaks. That was enough to make it
sound a whole lot nicer. Sure, there's always room for further improvement, but that's where I start
running into my sound designing limits.

### The Outro

When you make a cover of a song, you have to make it interesting. People are less likely to care if
you just end up mostly copying the original. Well, except when your goal _is_ to replicate the
original, so you can tell them what makes the song tick. <i class="text-dim">But then it's not a
cover any more, is it?</i>

Obviously, as I've just been talking about, a lot of the sounds in this cover are pretty different
and unique, while still capturing a similar dreamy vibe. That alone won't suffice, though. I
mentioned before that I changed up the last chord before the outro, and as you may know, the outro
itself is different, too.

In the original, it simply goes back to playing the same thing as the intro, in order to get a clean
loop. But this cover doesn't loop like that, which allows me to switch things up by replacing the
[keys](#keys) with the [arp](#arp). Doing that also makes the arp's special effects stand out more,
as they can be pretty subtle when there are drums and other instruments going on at the same time.

Now, that early version I just mentioned actually concludes on the <strong
	class="font-serif">iii</strong> <span class="font-serif text-dim">(A♭m)</span> chord. That could
maybe work in a way, but now, it instead resolves on the <strong
	class="font-serif">I</strong> <span class="font-serif text-dim">(E)</span> chord. Then, the tempo
proceeds to get slower and slower, before the instruments cut out and let the echoes do their thing.
And to wrap it up, those echoes <small>(assuming you are still listening closely enough...)</small>
are hit with a tape stop.<br /><small>Only happy endings for our Paige.</small>

You might say this still isn't enough, but unfortunately, I'm already starting to push my creativity
to its limits here. Coming up with new sections and stuff simply isn't my cup of tea. I'll leave
that to someone who actually studied music theory. If you want to have a go at it, again,
[the project files are up for grabs](https://fie.nl.tab.digital/s/q7ntrLN2bz9grzX?dir=/Music/Home%20Paige%20covers)!

### Gluing it all together

It's not lo-fi hip hop if it isn't lo-fi. _And that's a fact._ For this, I grouped all the tracks
together and added a side of **iZotope Vinyl**. You'll have no doubt noticed it in the last few
clips, since it's the source of the vinyl crackle. There's also a **soft clipper** on the master to
catch and slightly warm up any volume peaks.

<MainImage src={import("./assets/images/vinyl.avif")} alt="" createdIn="2026">
	The Vinyl settings for this cover. I also used Spindown at the very end.
</MainImage>

Finally, for that last touch of lo-fi, I slowed the final export down by **4.348%** in Audacity.
That's why the clips in this blog post sound a bit different compared to the actual cover. Well,
that and the many, many layers of randomness.

Why 4.348%, exactly? Well, I actually told you already; that's because this percentage is what's
needed to bring down the tempo to a nice round number of **88 bpm**. You really wouldn't want to end
up with fractional BPM here, because those are a meme.

</small>
